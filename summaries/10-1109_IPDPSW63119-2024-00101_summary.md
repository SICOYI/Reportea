## Abstract
This paper proposes a novel GNN-based preconditioner that integrates a Graph Neural Network model within a multi-level Domain Decomposition (DD) framework to solve large-scale Poisson problems. The preconditioner enhances Krylov iterative solvers, enabling convergence to any desired accuracy. It is GPU-executable, mesh-size agnostic, and incorporates a two-level approach for scalability. The method is validated numerically and benchmarked against a C++ legacy solver used in real CFD software.

## Research Problem
- Large-scale numerical simulations (e.g., solving the Poisson equation) are computationally expensive and bottleneck many CFD workflows.
- Traditional iterative solvers (Krylov methods) are efficient but CPU-bound and suffer from poor scalability with problem size.
- ML/GNN-based solvers leverage GPU parallelism but face two critical limitations:
  - **Generalization:** typically restricted to small, fixed-size meshes within the training distribution.
  - **Accuracy:** precision is capped by model capability and degrades on out-of-distribution inputs; no convergence guarantees exist.

---

## Methodology
- **Problem formulation:** Steady-state Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions, discretized via Finite Element Method (FEM) on unstructured meshes, yielding a sparse linear system `Au = b`.
- **Preconditioner design:** Mirrors the Additive Schwarz Method (ASM):
  - The global mesh is decomposed into overlapping sub-domains.
  - GNN models solve local sub-problems on each sub-domain in parallel on GPU.
  - Sub-domain sizes are adapted to GNN capabilities, enabling handling of arbitrarily large meshes.
- **Multi-level extension:** A two-level ASM variant adds a coarse problem correction (equation 7) to enforce weak scalability as the number of sub-domains grows.
- **Hybrid solver:** The GNN preconditioner (`M⁻¹_ASM`) is plugged into a Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) loop (Algorithm 1), guaranteeing convergence to any target tolerance.
- **Execution:** Sub-domain problems are batched and solved concurrently on GPU, exploiting parallel computation.

---

## Key Findings
- The GNN preconditioner significantly reduces the number of Krylov iterations required for convergence compared to unpreconditioned solvers.
- The hybrid solver achieves any desired level of accuracy, overcoming the fixed-precision limitation of pure ML approaches.
- The multi-level (two-level) formulation improves weak scalability with respect to the number of sub-domains.
- The method generalizes to meshes of varying sizes and shapes beyond the training distribution.
- Performance benchmarking against an optimized C++ legacy solver demonstrates competitive results, validating practical applicability in industrial CFD contexts.

---

## Contributions
1. **Novel GNN preconditioner** structured as a multi-level Additive Schwarz Method, integrating GNN sub-domain solvers into a rigorous mathematical framework.
2. **Convergence guarantee** via Krylov methods — unlike pure data-driven solvers, the hybrid approach converges to any precision.
3. **Scalability to large meshes** by decomposing problems into GNN-sized sub-domains, removing the fixed-mesh-size constraint of standard GNN solvers.
4. **GPU efficiency** through parallel batched sub-domain solving, retaining the GPU advantage of ML methods.
5. **Two-level coarse correction** for improved weak scalability across many sub-domains.
6. **Industrial validation** via comparison with a production-grade C++ CFD solver.

---

## Limitations
- The method is demonstrated primarily on the **Poisson equation**; generalization to other PDEs is not directly validated.
- GNN model accuracy on sub-domains still affects overall preconditioner quality (though Krylov convergence compensates).
- Out-of-distribution sub-domain geometries may degrade GNN sub-solver performance.
- Extending to very large numbers of sub-domains requires the coarse-level correction to remain effective; scalability beyond tested configurations is not fully characterized.
- The approach is compared to a C++ legacy solver but not against other modern ML-hybrid baselines in large-scale regimes.

---

## Conclusion
This work presents a principled hybrid methodology that bridges the gap between data-driven GNN solvers and classical iterative methods. By embedding a GNN model as the preconditioner within a multi-level Domain Decomposition framework, the approach inherits GPU efficiency and mesh flexibility from ML while restoring convergence guarantees via Krylov methods. The two-level extension addresses scalability. Results confirm numerical validity and competitive performance against legacy industrial solvers, making this a promising direction for large-scale scientific computing in engineering applications.
