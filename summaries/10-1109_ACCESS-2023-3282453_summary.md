## Title
A Machine Learning-Based Surrogate Finite Element Model for Estimating Dynamic Response of Mechanical Systems

## Authors
- Ali Hashemi — Florida Atlantic University, Boca Raton, FL, USA
- Jinwoo Jang (corresponding) — Florida Atlantic University, Boca Raton, FL, USA
- Javad Beheshti — Islamic Azad University, Tafresh, Iran

## Abstract
The study develops ML-based surrogate models for finite element analysis (FEA) to accelerate computation and avoid rerunning expensive simulations. Multiple ML regression algorithms — including decision trees and deep neural networks — are trained to predict time-varying nodal displacements of a 2D truss structure. Extreme gradient boosting decision trees are found to outperform all other tested algorithms.

## Research Problem
- FEA of dynamic mechanical systems is computationally expensive and time-consuming
- Repeated simulations are required during the lifespan of complex systems, making traditional FEA impractical
- ML surrogates for **dynamic** (time-varying) structural systems have been rarely assessed, with prior work focused mostly on static systems
- Predicting 28 outputs (14 nodal displacements × 2 directions) from 1-directional excitation loads poses under/overfitting challenges

## Methodology
- **System modeled:** 2D truss structure with 14 nodes and 28 displacement outputs
- **Input:** External excitation loadings in one direction
- **FEA role:** High-fidelity simulations used to generate training data
- **ML algorithms developed and compared:**
  - Decision Trees (DTs)
  - Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) — with varying hidden layers, units, activation functions, and gradient optimizers
  - Gradient Boosting Trees (e.g., XGBoost)
  - Bagging Trees (e.g., Random Forest)
  - Adaptive Boosting Trees
- Models trained on relatively small datasets compared to prior work

## Key Findings
- ML-based surrogate FE models can effectively predict dynamic displacement responses across the entire 2D truss structure
- **Extreme gradient boosting decision trees (XGBoost)** yielded the most precise results, outperforming all other ML algorithms
- Acceptable surrogate performance was achieved with smaller training datasets than previously required in comparable studies
- Under-fitting and over-fitting were addressed through architectural and hyperparameter tuning of DNN and tree ensemble models

## Contributions
- Development and benchmarking of multiple ML surrogate algorithms specifically for **dynamic** (transient) FEA — an under-explored area
- Demonstrated feasibility of predicting high-dimensional outputs (28 nodal displacements) with limited training data
- Provides a practical, low-cost alternative to repeated high-fidelity FE simulations in structural health monitoring and design
- Comparative analysis of DNN vs. tree ensemble methods in a time-varying structural context

## Limitations
- Study is limited to a **2D truss structure**; extension to 3D or more complex geometries may require significantly larger training datasets
- Excitation loading is applied in only **one direction**, limiting generalizability to multi-directional loading scenarios
- Higher-dimensional mechanical systems may demand substantially more training data due to increased model complexity
- The paper text appears truncated, so some experimental details and quantitative results may not be fully captured here

## Conclusion
The study successfully demonstrates that ML-based surrogate models — particularly extreme gradient boosting trees — can serve as efficient replacements for computationally expensive FEA in predicting dynamic structural responses. The approach significantly reduces computation time while maintaining predictive accuracy, offering practical utility in structural health monitoring, design optimization, and maintenance planning of mechanical systems.
