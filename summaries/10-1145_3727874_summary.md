## Title
Neurally Integrated Finite Elements for Differentiable Elasticity on Evolving Domains

## Authors
- Gilles Daviet (NVIDIA, France)
- Tianchang Shen (NVIDIA, Canada)
- Nicholas Sharp (NVIDIA, USA)
- David I.W. Levin (NVIDIA, Canada)

## Abstract
The paper presents an elastic simulator for domains defined as evolving implicit functions. It is efficient, robust, and differentiable with respect to both shape and material. The core innovation is training a small neural network to fit quadrature points for robust numerical integration on implicit grid cells. Combined with a Mixed Finite Element (FEM) formulation, it yields a smooth, fully differentiable simulation model linking implicit surface evolution to elastic response.

## Keywords
- Differentiable simulation
- Numerical integration
- Topology optimization
- Shape reconstruction
- Implicit geometry
- Finite element methods

## Research Problem
- 3D geometry reconstruction from images (e.g., via differentiable rendering) produces evolving implicit representations, but physically simulating and optimizing these shapes under deformation remains challenging.
- Existing FEM approaches require high mesh resolution, costly remeshing, or produce fuzzy interfaces.
- Fixed quadrature points introduce non-smoothness and ill-conditioning as the implicit surface evolves.
- No prior method supports simultaneous optimization of geometry, topology, and material properties in a fully differentiable pipeline.

## Methodology
- **Domain representation:** Geometry encoded as an implicit function on a regular grid.
- **Neural quadrature:** A small per-element neural network learns quadrature point locations and weights as a function of the underlying implicit shape, ensuring smooth, differentiable evolution alongside the geometry.
- **Mixed FEM formulation:** A rotation-aware mixed variational approach (extending Trusty et al. [2022]) supports high-order basis functions and maintains simulation stability across material stiffness values varying by up to four orders of magnitude.
- **Integration with reconstruction:** Combined with FlexiCubes [Shen et al. 2023] to connect reconstruction and physics in a single differentiable pipeline.
- **Optimization targets:** Quasi-static and dynamic simulation; image-guided material, shape, and topology optimization via differentiable rendering.

## Key Findings
- Neural quadrature enables smooth, differentiable integration over implicitly defined, evolving domains — avoiding the discontinuities of fixed quadrature schemes.
- The mixed FEM formulation prevents performance degradation under wildly varying material parameters.
- The method can simulate sub-grid thin features accurately on a coarse grid.
- End-to-end differentiability enables novel applications: image-guided topology optimization, simultaneous geometry and material recovery.
- Ablation confirms both components (mixed FEM + neural quadrature) are individually necessary for stable, robust results.

## Contributions
1. **Neural quadrature scheme** — first application of per-element neural networks to learn quadrature rules for evolving implicit domains in FEM.
2. **High-order mixed FEM on implicit grids** — avoids SVD per element at each sub-step; stable across extreme material parameter ranges.
3. **Fully differentiable elasticity simulator** — differentiable w.r.t. both shape (implicit function) and material parameters.
4. **Demonstrated pipeline** — combined with differentiable rendering for image-guided shape, material, and topology optimization without a shape prior.

## Limitations
- The method is scoped to elastic (deformable solid) simulation; extension to other physics (fracture, contact, fluids) is not addressed.
- Relies on an implicit grid discretization, which may introduce resolution constraints for very fine geometric detail beyond sub-grid feature handling.
- Neural network training per element adds overhead; scalability to very large domains is not fully characterized in the provided text.
- System identification assumes a parameterized material model; free-form material heterogeneity recovery may be constrained.

## Conclusion
The paper delivers a differentiable elastic simulator that natively operates on evolving implicit geometry by coupling a learned neural quadrature scheme with a robust mixed FEM formulation. This enables, for the first time, holistic optimization of shape, topology, and material properties within a single differentiable loop — directly addressing the gap between modern geometry reconstruction pipelines and physically-aware simulation for engineering and robotics applications.
