## Abstract
The paper proposes a novel GNN-based preconditioner that integrates a Graph Neural Network model within a multi-level Domain Decomposition framework to solve large-scale Poisson problems. The preconditioner enhances a Krylov iterative solver, forming a hybrid solver capable of achieving any desired accuracy. The GNN component runs on GPUs, handles meshes of arbitrary size and shape, and scales via a two-level Additive Schwarz Method. Performance is validated numerically and benchmarked against a C++ legacy solver used in real CFD software.

## Research Problem
- Solving large-scale Poisson PDEs is a major computational bottleneck in numerical simulations (e.g., CFD).
- Traditional solvers (CPU-based Krylov methods) guarantee convergence but scale poorly and cannot leverage GPU parallelism efficiently.
- Data-driven ML/GNN solvers exploit GPU parallelism but suffer from:
  - **Generalization failure**: constrained to small, fixed-size meshes within training distribution.
  - **Limited accuracy**: precision is capped by model capacity; degrades on out-of-distribution inputs.
- Existing hybrid DDM+ML approaches remain limited to small-scale, Cartesian domains with no convergence guarantees.

---

## Methodology
- **Problem formulation**: Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions discretized via Finite Element Method (FEM) into a sparse linear system `Au = b`.
- **Preconditioner design**: GNN-based preconditioner modeled after the multi-level Additive Schwarz Method (ASM):
  - The global domain is decomposed into overlapping sub-domains.
  - GNN models solve local sub-problems in parallel on GPU.
  - A two-level approach adds a coarse problem correction to improve weak scalability.
- **Hybrid solver**: The GNN preconditioner `M⁻¹_ASM` is plugged into a Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) Krylov method (Algorithm 1), which guarantees convergence to any target tolerance.
- **Scalability**: Sub-domain sizes are adapted to GNN capabilities; batched GPU execution processes multiple sub-domains concurrently.
- **Evaluation**: Numerical behavior validated experimentally; performance benchmarked against an optimized C++ legacy solver.

---

## Key Findings
- The GNN preconditioner significantly reduces the number of Krylov iterations required to reach a desired precision.
- The hybrid solver converges to any desired accuracy, overcoming the precision ceiling of pure ML approaches.
- The method generalizes to meshes of varying sizes and shapes beyond the training distribution.
- GPU execution of the GNN preconditioner provides competitive performance against the C++ legacy solver.
- The two-level approach improves weak scalability as the number of sub-domains increases.

---

## Contributions
1. **Novel GNN preconditioner** embedded in a multi-level Domain Decomposition framework — first of its kind at this scale.
2. **Convergence guarantee** via Krylov integration, resolving the accuracy limitation of pure data-driven solvers.
3. **Mesh-agnostic scalability**: the preconditioner adapts to arbitrary mesh sizes and shapes by tuning sub-domain sizes.
4. **GPU-accelerated** batched sub-domain solving, enabling practical use in industrial HPC pipelines.
5. **Two-level coarse correction** to enforce weak scalability with increasing numbers of sub-domains.
6. Comprehensive benchmarking against a production-grade C++ CFD solver.

---

## Limitations
- Evaluated primarily on the **Poisson equation**; generalization to other PDEs is not demonstrated in this paper.
- GNN model accuracy still bounds the quality of sub-domain solutions, affecting preconditioner efficiency even if convergence is guaranteed.
- Training data distribution may still influence how effective the preconditioner is for highly out-of-distribution geometries.
- The paper acknowledges that extending to general large-scale industrial settings with complex geometries remains an open challenge.

---

## Conclusion
The paper presents a hybrid solver that successfully bridges the gap between traditional numerical solvers and data-driven ML approaches. By embedding a GNN model as a preconditioner within a multi-level Additive Schwarz framework, the method achieves GPU-accelerated, mesh-agnostic preconditioning while retaining full convergence guarantees through Krylov iteration. The approach is competitive with legacy C++ solvers and lays a foundation for scalable, ML-enhanced PDE solving in industrial CFD contexts.
