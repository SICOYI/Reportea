## Title
A Machine Learning-Based Surrogate Finite Element Model for Estimating Dynamic Response of Mechanical Systems

## Authors
- Ali Hashemi — Florida Atlantic University, Boca Raton, FL, USA
- Jinwoo Jang (corresponding) — Florida Atlantic University, Boca Raton, FL, USA
- Javad Beheshti — Islamic Azad University, Tafresh, Iran

## Abstract
An ML-based surrogate framework for finite element analysis (FEA) is developed to accelerate simulation of dynamic mechanical systems. Multiple ML regression algorithms are trained to predict time-varying nodal displacements in a 2D truss structure, replacing costly repeated FE simulations. Extreme gradient-boosting decision trees (XGBoost) outperformed all other tested algorithms.

## Research Problem
- Full FEA simulations of dynamic mechanical systems are computationally expensive and time-consuming
- Running repeated high-fidelity FE models for structural health monitoring (SHM), sensor placement, and design evaluation is often impractical
- Predicting **time-varying** (transient) displacement responses using ML surrogates has been rarely explored, especially for multi-output (28 outputs: 14 nodes × 2 directions) problems
- Balancing under-fitting and over-fitting with limited training data and high-dimensional outputs is challenging

## Methodology
- **System modeled:** 2D truss structure subjected to external dynamic (transient) excitation loads
- **FEA:** Physics-based FE simulations used to generate training/testing datasets
- **Inputs:** External excitation loadings (in one direction)
- **Outputs:** 28 nodal displacements (14 nodes × 2 directions) over time
- **ML algorithms developed and compared:**
  - Decision Trees (DTs)
  - Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) — varied hidden layers, units, activation functions, and gradient optimizers
  - Random Forest (bagging ensemble)
  - Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost)
  - Gradient Boosting Regression Trees
  - Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost)
- Models trained on relatively small datasets compared to prior studies
- Overfitting/underfitting addressed through architectural tuning and ensemble methods

## Key Findings
- **XGBoost (extreme gradient-boosting decision trees) achieved the highest predictive accuracy** across all tested ML algorithms
- ML-based surrogate models successfully predicted time-varying displacement across the entire 2D truss structure
- Acceptable surrogate accuracy was achieved with **smaller training datasets** than previously reported in comparable research
- Deep neural networks showed sensitivity to architecture choices (layers, units, activation functions)

## Contributions
- Development of ML surrogate models specifically for **dynamic (transient)** FEA, extending prior work mostly limited to static systems
- Multi-output regression framework handling 28 simultaneous displacement outputs
- Systematic benchmarking of multiple ML regression paradigms (trees, ensembles, deep networks) for FEA surrogate use
- Demonstrated feasibility of surrogates with reduced training data requirements, lowering the cost of surrogate construction

## Limitations
- Study is limited to a **2D truss structure**; generalization to 3D or more complex geometries is not validated
- High-dimensional mechanical systems (beyond 2D) may require **substantially larger training datasets**
- Excitation loading is applied in **one direction only**, which may not reflect real-world multi-directional loading scenarios
- The paper text is partially truncated, so some experimental details and quantitative results may not be fully captured here

## Conclusion
The study demonstrates that ML-based surrogate models — particularly XGBoost — can effectively replace repeated FEA runs for predicting dynamic displacement responses in truss structures. The approach reduces computational cost while maintaining acceptable accuracy, even with relatively small training datasets. This positions ML surrogates as a practical alternative to high-fidelity FE simulations in structural health monitoring and design optimization workflows.
